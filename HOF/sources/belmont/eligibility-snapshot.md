# Belmont, San Mateo County, CA — Builder's Remedy / SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Eligibility Snapshot
Research date: 2026-09-05

## Current Compliance Status (Housing Element)

- Belmont's 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Housing Element went through a non-compliance → revision → compliance cycle:
  - HCD issued a Letter Requesting Revisions on the draft: **December 18, 2023**.
  - HCD issued a Letter of Substantial Compliance on a revised draft: **July 26, 2024**.
  - Belmont City Council adopted the revised 2023-2031 Housing Element: **September 10, 2024**.
  - HCD determined the adopted Housing Element **meets statutory requirements of State Housing Element Law: October 16, 2024**.
- **Current status as of September 2026: COMPLIANT**, and has been continuously compliant since October 16, 2024 (per the third-party Fair Housing Elements tracker, which lists Belmont as "In Compliance," and per HCD's own determination letter).
- This means the window during which Belmont was legally exposed to Builder's Remedy filings (any complete SB 330 preliminary application submitted while the housing element was NOT in substantial compliance) runs from the start of the 6th cycle review period through **October 16, 2024** at the latest, and arguably through September 10, 2024 (adoption) or July 26, 2024 (substantial compliance letter) depending on which HCD determination a given filing's timing is measured against.
- UNCONFIRMED: the exact date Belmont's *prior* (5th cycle / 2015-2023) housing element lapsed into non-compliance, which determines the true opening of the exposure window. Not independently verified in this pass.

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

**SB9 (ministerial lot splits / two-unit development):**
- Belmont has an adopted local implementing framework titled "Senate Bill 9 – Ministerial Urban Lot Splits & Two-Unit Development" (found via belmont-ca.granicus.com staff report/memo), confirming the city has established a local ministerial review process and objective standards rather than leaving SB9 unimplemented.
- Could not extract full ordinance text (PDF fetch returned corrupted/binary content). Based on general statewide SB9 constraints (which cap what any city, including Belmont, may impose): local objective standards may not physically preclude two units per lot or preclude either unit from being at least 800 sq ft; no local owner-occupancy requirement may be imposed beyond the state-mandated affidavit that the applicant intend to occupy one unit as principal residence for 3 years following an urban lot split (this 3-year owner-occupancy affidavit is a **state**, not Belmont-specific, requirement).
- UNCONFIRMED: whether Belmont's specific municipal code imposes additional objective design/development standards (setbacks, parking, lot coverage) beyond the state floor, and whether Belmont has any historic-district exclusion mapped locally. Not independently verified from primary ordinance text in this pass — flagged for follow-up directly against Belmont Municipal Code (not just the staff memo).

**SB35 (streamlined ministerial approval):**
- Belmont is confirmed to be a jurisdiction that **has not yet met its RHNA allocation for low- and very-low-income housing production**, which is the trigger that subjects a jurisdiction to SB35 (as amended/recodified in part by SB423) streamlined ministerial review.
- Per HCD's statewide SB35/SB423 methodology, jurisdictions in the Bay Area with insufficient progress toward Above-Moderate RHNA are generally subject to the **10% affordability tier** (with a 20% moderate-income option specific to the Bay Area) rather than the 50% tier (the 50% tier applies mainly to jurisdictions that failed to make progress on lower-income RHNA in specific ways defined by HCD's methodology).
- UNCONFIRMED: Belmont's precise placement (10% vs. 50% tier) on HCD's current SMAP (Streamlined Ministerial Approval Process) dashboard was not directly retrievable in this pass — the HCD statutory-determinations page points to a live dashboard ("updated daily beginning 6/30/2025") that could not be queried directly via fetch. This should be treated as UNCONFIRMED pending direct dashboard lookup.
- UNCONFIRMED: no specific SB35-labeled project application was found filed in Belmont in this research pass (the projects identified — Masonic Way, 796 Miramar Terrace, 815 Belmont Avenue — are documented as density-bonus/SB330/Builder's-Remedy applications, not SB35 applications).

**SB10 (local upzoning resolution for transit-rich/jobs-rich parcels):**
- No evidence found of Belmont having adopted an SB10 resolution or ordinance mapping transit-rich or jobs-rich parcels for up to 10-unit-per-parcel ministerial upzoning.
- Consistent with the broader statewide pattern — most CA cities, including Belmont, have NOT adopted SB10 resolutions since it is opt-in and requires an affirmative local legislative act (a city council resolution), which carries political cost that most councils have declined to spend.
- **Confirmed (by absence of any adoption record): Belmont has zero SB10 adoption as of September 2026.** This is a negative/absence finding — no direct "Belmont declines SB10" statement was found, but no adoption record, ordinance, staff report, or news coverage of an SB10 resolution turned up in this search pass. Treat as high-confidence but not a documentary certainty; flagged below.
