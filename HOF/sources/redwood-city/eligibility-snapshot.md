## 1. Current Compliance Status

**Redwood City's 6th Cycle (2023–2031) Housing Element is CERTIFIED / in substantial compliance,
and has been continuously since March 27, 2023.**

- HCD found the Housing Element in substantial compliance **March 27, 2023** — Redwood City was
  the **first jurisdiction in San Mateo County** to achieve this, and one of only 4 of 101 Bay
  Area jurisdictions compliant at that point in the cycle.
- Path to get there: initial draft submitted April 2022 → **rejected by HCD July 8, 2022**
  (cited lack of specificity/timelines) → revised and resubmitted (this is the ~3-round process
  most SMC jurisdictions needed; Redwood City needed only one further round, making it the
  *only* SMC jurisdiction that did **not** need 3+ rounds) → substantial compliance finding
  March 27, 2023.
- City has since promoted itself under this milestone (news release: "First City in San Mateo
  County with State Approved Housing Element," also received a "Prohousing Designation").
- **Live wrinkle as of Dec 2025 (material, not previously in our sources):** HCD sent Redwood
  City a formal **inquiry letter dated December 16, 2025** questioning the status of 6th Cycle
  Housing Element **implementation**, specifically the city's Program H1-10 (Previously
  Identified Sites) rezoning commitment. Under Gov. Code §65583(c)(1)(A), rezonings tied to a
  housing element found in substantial compliance within one year of the statutory deadline
  must generally be completed within **3 years of the Jan 31, 2023 deadline** (i.e., by
  ~Jan 31, 2026). This is an HCD status inquiry, not (yet) a decertification or non-compliance
  finding — but it signals HCD is watching whether Redwood City's rezoning program (not just the
  paper Housing Element) is actually complete on schedule.
- A related, separate 2025 effort — a citywide **Multifamily Zone / Objective Design Standards**
  RFP (deadline March 1, 2024) and subsequent zoning/design-standards work — appears to be part
  of the city's implementation program responding to this rezoning obligation.
- **Bottom line:** Compliant since March 2023, no lapse identified — but genuinely open question
  entering late 2026 whether the required rezonings (Program H1-10) closed out on time; if HCD
  ultimately finds implementation deficient, that is the mechanism by which a currently-compliant
  city could become exposed to Builder's Remedy risk later. Flag for a follow-up check in early
  2026 for HCD's response/resolution to the Dec 16, 2025 letter.

## 3. SB35 — Eligible? Local Objective Design Standards?

- **Eligibility:** SB35 streamlining applies based on a jurisdiction's progress toward its RHNA
  by income category (not overall Housing Element compliance) — no Redwood-City-specific SB35
  determination or published eligibility percentage was found in this pass. No confirmed SB35
  project filed in Redwood City was found (consistent with the broader San Mateo County finding
  in `reports/san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md` that Builder's Remedy, not SB35, dominates
  local streamlining activity in this county).
- **Objective design standards:** Redwood City has been actively developing these. An RFP for
  "Objective Design Standards Services" (residential/mixed-use projects of 5+ units, emphasis on
  massing/modulation/articulation) was issued with a **March 1, 2024** response deadline. This
  appears to be the same implementation-program work tied to the Housing Element rezoning
  obligation (see §1). **Unconfirmed:** whether these standards have since been formally adopted
  by ordinance/resolution (a prerequisite for a city to apply its own objective standards to
  SB35 projects — absent adoption, SB35 applicants need only meet general-plan/zoning objective
  standards that already exist). No adoption-date confirmation found this pass.

## 4. SB10 — Locally Adopted/Mapped, or Not?

**Not adopted.** No evidence Redwood City has passed an SB10 ordinance up-zoning any parcel to
10 units/parcel in transit-rich or urban-infill areas.

**Materially relevant instead — SB79 (2026), a different and more recent transit-housing law:**
Redwood City City Council voted **unanimously in July 2026** to comply with **SB79** (effective
July 1, 2026), which authorizes multistory housing within a half-mile of major transit stops
(heavy/commuter/light rail or rapid bus) via a "transit-oriented development zone" mechanism.
The Planning Commission recommended, and Council approved, **adopting the state default
standards as-is now**, while **concurrently developing a local alternative TOD plan targeted for
spring 2027**. This is a live, in-progress local customization window — worth tracking, as the
local alternative plan could tighten or loosen the state default before it's locked in.

Do not conflate SB10 (2021, voluntary local up-zoning tool, essentially unused statewide) with
SB79 (2026, transit-proximity zoning, which Redwood City has actually acted on) — the report
should treat SB10 as inapplicable/inactive here and flag SB79 as the operative current-law event.

## 5. Builder's Remedy Projects — Table

**Redwood City has zero Builder's Remedy projects, and is not currently exposed to Builder's
Remedy eligibility, because it has held a certified Housing Element continuously since March
2023** (Builder's Remedy applies only to jurisdictions without an HCD-compliant Housing Element
at the time of application submittal). This is explicitly confirmed by direct reporting
(Redwood City Pulse): the city "is not subject to the builder's remedy... a penalty that affects
cities without a legally compliant housing element."

| — none found — | — | N/A | No Builder's Remedy application has been filed, is pending, or has been approved/rejected in Redwood City in this research pass. City's compliant status since 2023 forecloses BR eligibility for any project filed after that date. |

This stands in sharp contrast to the county's non-compliant jurisdictions (Pacifica, Menlo Park,
Half Moon Bay, Daly City, San Bruno, Belmont, Atherton, Woodside, unincorporated county — per
`reports/san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md`), several of which do have live/contested BR
pipelines (e.g., Pacifica's Coastal Crest Residences, rejected 5-0 by Planning Commission Aug 3
2026; Menlo Park's 80 Willow Rd and Willow-Sunset proposals). Redwood City is the county's
compliance outlier, not a BR battleground.

**Caveat:** if the Dec 2025 HCD implementation inquiry (§1) ultimately results in HCD finding
Redwood City's Housing Element implementation deficient, or if AB 1893's "substantial compliance"
redefinition (effective Jan 1 2025, requiring an actual HCD/court compliance determination) were
applied retroactively in a way that unwound the 2023 finding, that could theoretically re-open BR
exposure — but no evidence found this pass that this is happening or imminent. Purely a
watch-item, not a current fact.

## 6. 603 Jefferson Ave — Current Status

**This is now a live, larger, DIFFERENT project than the litigated 91→68-unit condo case —
disambiguation confirmed this pass.**

- **Historical case (pre-2016-ish):** "Redwood City Residents for Responsible Development"
  appealed Planning Commission approval of an 8-story/91-unit condo project at 603 Jefferson
  Ave; City Council denied the appeal but approved a scaled-back 6-story/68-unit version (a
  23-unit loss). This was reported as the first project subject to the Dec. 2015 Affordable
  Housing Impact Fee Ordinance (No. 2417). **No evidence found that this 68-unit condo version
  was ever built** — construction status of that specific 2015-era approved project is
  unconfirmed/unresolved (SOURCES.md already flagged this as an open thread).
- **Current (2025-2026) project at the same address:** Sobrato Organization, on behalf of
  property owner Redwood City School District, filed **updated plans in September 2025** for
  **603 Jefferson Avenue & 750 Bradford Street** jointly — a much larger mixed-use redevelopment:
  an office tower up to 126 ft/8 floors (replacing/relocating ~20,500 sq ft of RCSD headquarters
  space) and a separate apartment building up to 109 ft/10 floors, plus parking for 236 cars and
  70 bicycles, on a combined ~1.25-acre parcel. Demolition target: the existing 2-story RCSD
  headquarters and low-slung commercial building.
- **Status as of this pass:** "Updated plans filed" (Sept 2025) is the most recent confirmed
  status. **No confirmed approval hearing date, entitlement decision, or construction
  start/timeline was found** — the Sept 2025 SF YIMBY coverage explicitly states cost and
  construction timeline "have yet to be shared." Not the same permitting track as the historical
  litigated project; this is presumably processed under the city's newer Downtown Precise
  Plan / "Gatekeeper Projects" framework (per SOURCES.md's unconfirmed lead) rather than a
  standalone post-litigation ordinance — this framework connection remains unconfirmed and would

- **Unit-count comparison caveat for the calling report:** if `san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md`

## 7. 2025-2026 Ballot Measures / Ordinance Changes Affecting Housing Approval

**Live, significant: a citizen-initiated rent control / tenant-protection ballot measure is
headed to the November 2026 ballot** (does not affect zoning/entitlement approval directly, but
is the dominant 2025-2026 housing-policy fight in the city and materially affects the housing
production/investment climate):

- Backed by Faith in Action; filed as a rent control ballot initiative in **October 2025**
  (a refiling — an earlier version had been filed previously, per "again" in the Pulse headline).
- City Council initially **deferred adoption**, seeking fuller review (per California Apartment
  Association coverage, June 2026 Pulse coverage) rather than adopting the citizen initiative
  outright.
- As of **July 2026**, Council voted unanimously to place the measure on the **November 3, 2026**
  ballot.
- **Substance:** rent-increase cap at 60% of local CPI increase (capped at 5% max annual
  increase), a **rollback of rents to Oct. 29, 2025 levels**, tougher eviction standards, higher
  relocation payments in some cases, a new city-run rental oversight program. Costa-Hawkins
  exempts single-family homes, condos, and most post-1995 multifamily — so the measure would
  cover roughly **20% of the city's housing stock**.
- **Separate, confirmed, adopted-not-ballot ordinance change:** the **SB79 compliance vote,
  July 2026** (see §4) — adopting state transit-housing default standards while drafting a local
  alternative TOD plan for spring 2027. This is a real, adopted (not ballot) zoning-relevant
  change with a pending second phase.
- **Also in-progress (adoption unconfirmed):** the Objective Design Standards / Multifamily Zone
  program (see §1, §3) tied to Housing Element implementation — appears to still be moving
  through Council in 2025-2026, exact adoption date/ordinance number not confirmed this pass.

No measure found this pass that would relax/streamline entitlement review (e.g., no local
Builder's-Remedy-preemption measure, no downzoning ballot measure) — the one confirmed 2025-2026
ballot fight is rent control, which is a post-entitlement landlord/tenant regulation, not a
zoning/approval-process change.
