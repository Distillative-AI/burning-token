# Town of Hillsborough, San Mateo County — Builder's Remedy / SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Eligibility Snapshot
Research date: 2026-09-05

## Current Compliance Status (Housing Element)

- Hillsborough is in its 6th RHNA cycle (2023-2031), with a RHNA allocation of 554 units.
- HCD issued a letter dated **November 30, 2023** finding the Town's *draft* Housing Element in **substantial compliance** with State Housing Element Law, conditioned on adoption without material change.
- The Town Council held a special meeting on **December 20, 2023** to certify the associated EIR and adopt the Housing Element; the Final Housing Element document was made available **December 14, 2023**.
- General web search results (non-HCD-portal) characterize Hillsborough's Housing Element as HCD-**compliant** for the current (6th) cycle, citing it as "one of few jurisdictions" to have reached this status among small, low-density Peninsula towns.
- UNCONFIRMED: The exact date of HCD's *final* post-adoption certification letter (as opposed to the Nov 30, 2023 pre-adoption substantial-compliance finding) was not independently located in this pass — the Town's own housing-element page does not show a follow-up certification letter, and HCD's housing-element compliance dashboard/portal (https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/housing-open-data-tools/housing-element-implementation-and-apr-dashboard) was not directly queried (fetch not attempted in this session; recommend a direct pull of Hillsborough's row from HCD's Housing Element Compliance Report dataset at https://data.ca.gov/dataset/housing-element-compliance-report to pin the exact certification date and confirm no subsequent decertification).
- UNCONFIRMED: No specific record of Hillsborough housing-element **litigation** (e.g., an AG enforcement action, builder's-remedy lawsuit naming Hillsborough, or a court decertifying the element) was found in this research pass, despite the brief's premise that Hillsborough has "a notable history of housing element litigation/non-compliance." This may refer to an earlier RHNA cycle (5th cycle or prior) not surfaced by current search results, or to reputational/anecdotal characterization rather than a documented legal action — flagging as UNCONFIRMED rather than asserting a specific case.

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

**SB9 (lot splits / two-unit developments):**
- The Town adopted a local urgency ordinance on **December 13, 2021**, and separately, Ordinance No. 781 (dated in Town records to **January 20, 2021** per one source — this date pre-dates SB9's own January 1, 2022 effective date, so it is likely a Town Council resolution-numbering artifact or a typo in the source document; treat as UNCONFIRMED) established **objective development standards** for SB9 projects, codified at Hillsborough Municipal Code Chapter 16.44 (SB9 Urban Lot Splits) and Chapter 17.54 (SB9 Two-Unit Projects).
- Standards apply the Town's existing large-lot residential development standards (building size, lot coverage, setbacks, height) to SB9 projects **except where they would conflict with SB9's own mandated minimums** (state law preempts local standards that would otherwise physically preclude the two units or lot split SB9 guarantees).
- Cited application-level standards: lot-split results in no more than 2 parcels, each ≥1,200 sq ft; both parcels must have or provide access to public right-of-way; at least 1 off-street parking space per unit.
- Given Hillsborough's underlying zoning is very-low-density/large-lot (commonly 0.5–1+ acre minimums per the brief), SB9's ministerial lot-split right applies statewide regardless of local minimum lot size — SB9 overrides local minimum lot size for purposes of the split itself, though the Town's objective standards otherwise still constrain building envelope, setbacks and height. UNCONFIRMED: no data found in this pass on how many SB9 applications (lot splits or two-unit projects) have actually been filed or approved in Hillsborough to date, or whether the very large parcel sizes have effectively resulted in zero or near-zero SB9 utilization.

**SB35 (streamlined ministerial approval):**
- Search results indicate Hillsborough is **not currently on HCD's SB35-eligible determination list** — i.e., not subject to SB35's streamlined ministerial process at either the 10% or 50% affordability tier, consistent with the Town's Housing Element being found in compliance (SB35 eligibility/tier is driven by RHNA progress and housing element compliance status; a compliant jurisdiction reporting adequate progress is generally not SB35-eligible).
- UNCONFIRMED: This was inferred from a general search summary, not a direct pull of HCD's SB35 Statewide Determination Summary PDF (https://www.hcd.ca.gov/community-development/housing-element/docs/SB35_StatewideDeterminationSummary.pdf) or the SB35 Determination Map dataset (https://data.ca.gov/dataset/streamlined-ministerial-approval-process-sb35-determination-map). Recommend directly querying that dataset for Hillsborough's row to confirm tier/eligibility with a citable primary source.
- No SB35 project filings or approvals for Hillsborough were found in this research pass.

**SB10 (transit-rich/jobs-rich upzoning, up to 10 units/parcel):**
- **Confirmed via the Town's own SB9 & SB10 page** (https://www.hillsborough.net/604/SB-9-10): "The Town currently has no plan to adopt such an ordinance." Hillsborough has **not adopted** an SB10 resolution/ordinance mapping transit-rich or jobs-rich parcels. SB10 is optional/enabling legislation, not a mandate, and Hillsborough has explicitly declined to use it — consistent with the general statewide pattern of very low adoption by cities.
