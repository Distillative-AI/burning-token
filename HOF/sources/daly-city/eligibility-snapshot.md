## 1. Current Compliance Status

**Best-evidence conclusion: Daly City's 2023-2031 Housing Element is very likely NOT YET
HCD-certified as "in compliance," but the city has taken the single largest concrete step
toward compliance available — an emergency citywide rezoning ordinance passed Nov 12, 2024 —
specifically to cure the deficiency HCD identified. A definitive "certified as of [date]"
letter was not located this pass.**

Evidence trail, in order of authority:

- **HCD's own review letters (primary, dated)**: HCD's Sept 26, 2023 letter and a "Second
  Comment Letter on Revised Draft Housing Element" (https://dalycity.org/DocumentCenter/View/9544)
  both found the draft **not yet substantially compliant** and required revisions, principally
  around **sites inventory / rezoning capacity** — as of the May 2024 HCD Submittal Draft 3, the
  city's own filing showed existing zoning capacity for only **3,985 units against a 4,838-unit
  RHNA allocation** — a real shortfall requiring rezoning, not a paperwork gap.
- **AB 1398 penalty clock is in play**: per an MTC/OBAG summary sheet
  (mtc.ca.gov/.../4a_24_1303_1_Summary_Sheet_OBAG_HE_Compliance.pdf), Daly City is explicitly
  flagged as **not in compliance**, and AB 1398 means a jurisdiction that missed the statutory
  deadline by more than a year **cannot be found in compliance until the required rezonings to
  cover the shortfall are actually completed** — adoption of the Housing Element text alone
  is not sufficient; the sites-capacity rezoning has to be done and effective.
- **The cure action taken**: City Council passed an **emergency ordinance Nov 12, 2024**
  (per Housing Leadership Council of San Mateo County,
  https://hlcsmc.org/2024/11/21/daly-city-rezones-for-denser-more-affordable-homes/) that:
  - raised height limits from 36 ft to **120 ft** in multifamily zones (175 ft / 15 stories in
    the new C-MU zone)
  - **doubled density citywide**; max-density areas now require a **minimum of 120 du/ac with
    no upper cap**
  - eliminated maximum lot-coverage restrictions in multifamily zones
  - rezoned ~12 acres near Seton Medical Center for 60-120 du/ac (500+ potential units)
  - explicitly framed by the city/HLC as "implemented as part of its efforts to create a
    compliant housing element" — i.e., this is the AB 1398 shortfall-cure rezoning.
- **Secondary aggregator ("Housing Readiness Report")** states Daly City "has a Housing
  Element certified by HCD for the current planning cycle (2023-2031)" — but this source gave
  the *identical boilerplate sentence* for every jurisdiction queried in this research session
  (it appears to be a templated compliance-tracker page, not a live per-city HCD certification
  feed) and could not be corroborated against an actual HCD certification letter or dated press
  release. **Treat this specific claim as low-confidence / possibly a templated/stale default,
  not confirmed.**
- **No 2025-2026 news article, HCD letter, or council resolution announcing final certification
  was found** in this pass despite multiple targeted searches. The most recent *dated* primary
  evidence (Nov 2024 rezoning + the pre-rezoning May 2024 HCD comment letter) is consistent with
  Daly City being **mid-cure** — rezoned to add capacity, but no located record confirms HCD
  issued a subsequent "found in compliance" letter following the Nov 2024 rezoning.

**Contradicts prior research assumption**: the fact-check-verification.md report's Sept-2024
"8 non-compliant jurisdictions including Daly City" framing likely still held through late 2024,
but Daly City is now distinguishable from purely-stalled jurisdictions (Atherton, Woodside) —
it has taken the specific AB 1398 curative action (rezoning) that would restore eligibility for
compliance findings once HCD reviews the post-rezoning sites inventory. Whether that review has
concluded is the single biggest open item from this pass.

## 2. SB9/35/10 Status

- **SB9**: Daly City has a dedicated **"SB9 Application Materials"** page
  (https://dalycity.org/1185/SB9-Application-Materials), indicating the city processes SB9
  urban-lot-splits/two-unit developments via its own application forms/checklist rather than
  silence-implies-state-default. This is consistent with **state-mandated ministerial SB9
  processing with a locally-published application packet**, not evidence of a *locally adopted
  SB9 ordinance* that alters state defaults (e.g., no evidence found of a Daly City-specific
  SB9 overlay/ordinance comparable to San Mateo's Two-Unit Development Overlay). Treat as
  **state-default SB9 eligibility, city-administered via standard forms** — not a custom
  local ordinance.
- **Objective Design Standards**: Daly City has TWO published objective design standards
  documents (https://www.dalycity.org/1280/Objective-Design-Standards): (1) citywide standards
  for single-family and multifamily residential, and (2) separate standards for the new
  Commercial Mixed-Use (C-MU) zone. These are the standards that let SB9/SB35/HAA-covered
  projects be reviewed only against objective (non-discretionary) criteria. **Adoption date not
  confirmed this pass** — the page content didn't surface a date, and this is a reasonable
  target for the outstanding Municode/live-browser pull already flagged in SOURCES.md.
- **SB35**: No SB35-specific Daly City page or project was found. Given RHNA shortfall/
  non-compliance exposure historically, Daly City would sit in the deeper (more permissive)
  SB35 affordability-set-aside tier for qualifying infill projects. **No confirmed SB35 project
  filed in Daly City found this pass** (consistent with prior research's finding that Builder's
  Remedy, not SB35, dominates non-compliant-jurisdiction streamlining activity countywide).
- **SB10**: No evidence found of Daly City adopting an SB10 transit-density resolution or
  mapping SB10-eligible parcels. Consistent with the countywide pattern noted in
  san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md (no county jurisdiction has adopted SB10; eligibility
  exists everywhere as a local option, uptake is zero). **No contradiction of prior research
  here — reconfirms zero SB10 uptake, this time for Daly City specifically.**

## 4. Recent Ballot/Ordinance Changes (2025-2026)

Confirmed via primary-source PDF extraction (`curl`+`pdftotext`, per SOURCES.md §3 — already
verified in this project's chronology, restated here for the eligibility-snapshot context):

- **Zoning Change ZC-02-26-017000** (citywide), Planning Commission hearing **March 3, 2026**,
  City Council introduction **March 23, 2026**, second reading **scheduled April 13, 2026**
  (confirmation of actual passage + assigned ordinance number still outstanding per SOURCES.md).
  Key substantive changes:
  - **Use Permit and Design Review approvals extended from prior expiration to 5 years**
    (Planning Commission amended staff's original 3-year proposal up to 5 years).
  - Time-extension window for expired permits lengthened from 6 months to **2 years**.
  - **Condominium subdivisions exempted from requiring a use permit** (Ch. 17.37.030) — only
    conversions/stock cooperatives/communal ownership still require one.
  - Front-yard paving rules tightened (triangular landscaping requirement, corner-lot side-yard
    paving prohibition) — a minor restriction, not a housing-production lever.
  - Building-height definition restored for non-C-MU zones (a 2025 General Plan drafting gap
    fix, not a new policy).
  - **Explicitly framed by staff's own Discussion section** as implementing the 2024 Housing
    Element's commitment to reduce discretionary-review barriers — i.e., this March 2026
    ordinance is a direct downstream housing-element-implementation action, same lineage as the
    Nov 2024 emergency rezoning.
- **The Nov 12, 2024 emergency rezoning ordinance** (see §1 above) is the more consequential
  2024-2026 change for RHNA/eligibility purposes: 120 ft height limits (175 ft in C-MU),
  doubled density, 120 du/ac minimum with no cap in max-density areas, eliminated lot-coverage
  caps, ~12-acre Seton Medical Center rezone.
- **No ballot measure specific to Daly City housing was found** in this pass (unlike San Mateo's
  Measure T or Menlo Park's Nov 2026 parking-lot measure, both covered in the county-level
  report) — Daly City's housing-capacity changes have run entirely through Council ordinance,
  not the ballot box, as of this research.
