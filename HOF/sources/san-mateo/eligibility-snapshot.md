## 1. Current Compliance Status

- **2023-2031 Housing Element adopted:** May 2024 (adopted by City Council; HAC lawsuit filed
  within ~24 hours per prior research, confirmed by SF Standard / CBS SF / Davis Vanguard
  coverage).
- **HCD certification:** HCD found the adopted Housing Element in **substantial compliance**
  with State Housing Element Law by letter **July 23, 2024**.
- **Housing Action Coalition (HAC) v. City of San Mateo lawsuit — RESOLVED.** HAC and
  Farella Braun + Martel LLP sued alleging the site inventory relied on non-viable/unrealistic
  sites (over half the low-income RHNA obligation, per prior research framing) — specifically
  challenging the **Bridgepointe Shopping Center parking lot (~8 acres)** and **Borel Shopping
  Center** sites, among others. **Settlement agreement reached ~July 2025**: the City agreed to
  (a) remove most of the challenged sites, including Bridgepointe and Borel, from the opportunity-
  sites inventory, (b) recalculate site capacity estimates to better account for the General Plan
  and **Measure T** upzoning, and (c) add replacement sites to the inventory, including some with
  active development proposals already in progress.
- **Post-settlement Housing Element Amendment:** City Council adopted a Housing Element
  Amendment on **February 23, 2026**, implementing the settlement's inventory changes. HCD issued
  a **Housing Element Amendment Certification Letter on June 1, 2026**, confirming the amended
  Housing Element meets state law.
- **Net status as of Sept 2026: City of San Mateo is HCD-compliant**, on its second
  (post-litigation-settlement) certification, not merely the original May 2024 adoption. This
  materially updates prior research that left the HAC suit's outcome as an open/unresolved
  question.
- For contrast, **San Mateo County** (the unincorporated county, a separate jurisdiction) adopted
  its own amended Housing Element April 21, 2026, HCD-confirmed May 18, 2026 — do not conflate
  City of San Mateo's compliance history with the County's; they are on separate, only loosely
  related tracks.

## 2. SB9/35/10 Status

**SB 9 (2021 — by-right lot splits / up to 2 units per parcel, ≤4 units total).**
- City of San Mateo adopted a **Two-Unit Development Residential Overlay District** (San Mateo
  Municipal Code Chapter 27.21, sections 27.21.010–27.21.070), implemented via **Ordinance
  2022-10**.
- Standards (per code + city SB9 guidance page, `cityofsanmateo.org/4598`): 4 ft side/rear
  setbacks (front setback follows underlying zone); height caps 24 ft plateline / 32 ft peak for
  attached units, 16 ft plateline / 24 ft peak for detached units; 1 parking space per SB9 unit
  unless exempt (transit proximity / accommodation).
- **Still in effect as of Sept 2026** — no repeal or material amendment found. A related **Zone
  Text Amendment 2023-01** (SB9 Two-Unit Development, referenced in a CEQAnet filing) suggests at
  least one technical amendment cycle occurred post-adoption; exact amendment content not
  independently confirmed this pass (CEQAnet record found by search only, not fetched/read).

**SB 35 (2017 — streamlined ministerial approval for qualifying infill, no CEQA/discretionary
hearing).**
- City has adopted **Citywide Objective Design Standards (ODS)** for multi-family and
  residential mixed-use development, approved by City Council **November 20, 2023**, effective
  **January 3, 2024** (Ordinance 2023-12), explicitly in response to Gov't Code §65913.4 (the
  SB35 streamlining statute). ODS lets the Zoning Administrator approve qualifying projects up to
  25 units under objective standards, bypassing discretionary review.
- This followed the **CaRLA v. City of San Mateo** Housing Accountability Act case (First
  District Court of Appeal, published decision Sept 10, 2021; City settled Oct 2021, $450,000 to
  petitioners, did not appeal) that established San Mateo needed genuinely objective standards.
- SB35 numeric eligibility (i.e., whether the city currently sits in the >50% or <50%
  above-moderate-RHNA-progress tier that sets the 10%-vs-50% affordability threshold for SB35
  projects) was **not independently confirmed this pass** — the city's own ODS page does not
  state an eligibility percentage, and HCD's SB35 determination tracker was not fetched. Flagged
  as unconfirmed below.

**SB 10 (2021 — local-option, up to 10 units/parcel near transit, CEQA-exempt zoning action) —
STATUS CHANGE FROM PRIOR RESEARCH.**
- Prior research (`san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md`) stated: city "has mapped qualifying
  transit geography (Caltrain stations, El Camino Real corridor) but no jurisdiction in the
  county has actually adopted SB10 — eligibility exists, uptake is zero," and named "mandatory
  SB10 adoption" as an available high-leverage, low-effort policy lever.
- **This is now superseded by SB 79 (2025, Wiener), a mandatory (not opt-in) state upzoning law**
  signed by Governor Newsom (signing reported Oct 10, 2025 per SF Standard). SB79 applies
  **statewide in "urban transit counties," including San Mateo County**, and — unlike SB10, which
  required local council adoption — takes effect **automatically July 1, 2026, unless the city
  adopts its own HCD-approved ordinance or local TOD alternative plan before that date.**
  Reported density tiers: up to 9 stories immediately adjacent to any Caltrain/BART stop, 7
  stories within a quarter-mile, 6 stories between a quarter- and half-mile.
  - City of San Mateo has published its own "Public Guidance: New State Rules for Development
    Near Transit (Senate Bill 79)" document (`cityofsanmateo.org/DocumentCenter/View/99605/`),
    confirming the city is treating SB79 as directly applicable — but the fetch tool could not
    extract readable text from this PDF (binary/compressed stream). **Unconfirmed this pass:
    whether San Mateo intends to adopt its own local TOD alternative plan before July 1, 2026, or
    will simply accept the state default tiers.** This is the single most consequential open
    question for the eligibility geography this project cares about (Caltrain stations, El
    Camino Real corridor) — recommend a live-session PDF read or direct city Planning Dept.
    contact.
  - SB10 itself has not been found to have been formally repealed — it remains theoretically
    available as a local-option tool — but SB79's mandatory statewide mechanism now moots the
    "voluntary uptake is zero" framing for San Mateo's core transit geography: the same
    Caltrain-station / El Camino Real corridor logic previously cited as the SB10 opportunity is
    now the exact geography SB79 forces upzoning onto, on a mandatory clock (July 1, 2026).

## 4. Recent Ballot/Ordinance Changes

- **Measure T (Nov 5, 2024, ~59% yes)** — City of San Mateo General Plan Amendment. Amended the
  1991 voter-initiative height/density cap (**Measure Y**) to allow height/density increases in
  10 specified areas (downtown, El Camino Real, areas near the city's 3 Caltrain stations —
  reported up to 8 stories vs. the prior 5-story cap in core areas). Passed to help meet the
  ~7,000-unit 2023-31 RHNA allocation.
- **Implementing Ordinance 2024-10** — "Measure T – General Plan Land Use Element" — codifies the
  Measure T changes into the General Plan.
- **Ordinance 2025-05** — "Zoning Code Amendments and Below Market Rate Program Update," part of
  the **Bundled Zoning Code Amendments (Phase 1 / PA-2025-002)** for Housing Element (2023-2031)
  and General Plan consistency with Measure T. Two ordinances were introduced March 17, 2025 and
  **adopted April 7, 2025**; amends multiple Title 27 (Zoning) chapters — housing production
  support, affordable-housing compliance options, planning-application procedures, and minimum
  densities for residential mixed-use projects. (Second companion ordinance's exact number not
  independently confirmed this pass — search results consistently surfaced 2025-05 but not its
  companion; flagged below.)
- **November 2026 City ballot measures (Measure H, Measure F)** — confirmed via direct fetch of
  `cityofsanmateo.org/ballotmeasure`: **Measure H is a ¼-cent transactions/use sales tax measure**
  and **Measure F is a charter amendments measure**. **Neither appears to be a housing/zoning
  measure** based on the fetched page content — no follow-on NIMBY or pro-housing ballot measure
  was found for the Nov 2026 City of San Mateo ballot. (Note: page content was fetched via a
  small-model text extraction, not a full human read of linked Impartial Analysis/FAQ documents —
  treat "not housing-related" as provisional, not certain; a live session should confirm by
  reading the linked analyses directly.)
- **San Mateo County** (separate jurisdiction) considered, then **decided against**, placing an
  affordable-housing pre-approval ballot measure (up to 1%/year of existing units, bypassing
  voter approval) on a recent county ballot — this is a County, not City, matter and did not
  proceed.
- **2025 Triennial Building Code Update (Ordinance 2025-09)** — adopted Nov 3, 2025, effective
  ~Dec 2025; adopts the 2025 California Existing Building Code (Title 24, Part 10). Not
  housing-policy-specific but relevant to construction-cost/compliance baseline for any project
  filed after this date.
