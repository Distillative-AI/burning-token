## Current Compliance Status

**HOUSING ELEMENT: NOW CERTIFIED / COMPLIANT** — this is a change from the prior finding
of non-compliant as of Sept 2024.

Full timeline (confirmed via `smcgov.org/planning/san-mateo-county-housing-element-update-2023-2031`
and corroborating search results):

- Jan 20, 2023 — draft Housing Element submitted to HCD.
- Apr 20, 2023 — HCD comment letter (requested modifications) — county was **non-compliant**
  from this point through the dates below.
- Jan 2024 — updated draft.
- Apr 23, 2024 — Board of Supervisors adopts (1st round) — still not HCD-consistent.
- Jul 5, 2024 — HCD 2nd comment letter (still non-compliant) — **this covers the "non-compliant
  as of Sept 2024" finding from prior research; confirmed accurate for that date.**
- Feb 5, 2025 — county resubmits.
- **Mar 7, 2025 — HCD determines Housing Element consistent with State law.**
- Mar 25, 2025 — Board of Supervisors re-adopts.
- Mar 2026 — further draft amendments (incl. HE 11.2 density increase 120→150 units/acre
  non-coastal), public review through Mar 19, 2026.
- Apr 21, 2026 — Board of Supervisors adopts amended Housing Element.
- **May 18, 2026 — HCD confirms the (amended) Housing Element "meets the requirements of
  State law."** This is the current, latest compliance milestone.

**Net: the county has been HCD-compliant since March 7, 2025** (initial certification), through
a further amendment cycle re-certified May 18, 2026. This directly **contradicts/updates** any
report text still stating the county is non-compliant — verify and update
`reports/san-mateo-housing-structural-moats.md` / `reports/fact-check-verification.md`
accordingly (see "Contradictions" below).

**Caveat — rezoning program (HE 11.2) still incomplete:** the Housing Element's own rezoning
program addressing the county's site-inventory shortfall (density increase, targeted parcels)
is still in progress ("scheduled to begin Summer 2025, continue through 2026"); as of this pass
it had not been reported complete. This matters because Builder's Remedy exposure is tied to
"substantial compliance" status, not merely Housing Element certification — see next section.

**Builder's-Remedy-relevant nuance:** per Coastside Buzz (citing county Planning Director Steve
Monowitz at a May 13, 2026 Planning Commission meeting), Builder's Remedy **pre-applications
already filed are legally protected and must be processed regardless**, but **once the county
reaches "substantial compliance" with HCD, new BR applications will no longer be accepted**.
The exact date/trigger of "substantial compliance" (a distinct, likely later or parallel
determination from the Housing Element certification above) was **not confirmed** in this pass —
flagged as UNCONFIRMED below.

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

### SB9 (urban lot splits / two-unit developments)
- **CONFIRMED still in force, ministerial review, with owner-occupancy affidavit** — matches
  prior research. County program pages: `smcgov.org/planning/senate-bill-9-sb-9`,
  `.../urban-lot-splitsubdivision-allowed-ca-senate-bill-9-sb9`,
  `.../housing-development-allowed-ca-senate-bill-9-sb9`.
- Owner-occupancy requirement: owner must sign an affidavit committing to occupy one unit as
  principal residence for a minimum of **3 years** from lot-split approval.
- Standard SB9 exclusions apply (no demolition/alteration of affordable, rent-controlled, or
  recently-tenant-occupied housing).
- **No 2025/2026 state-level SB9 amendment was confirmed** in this pass (a bill to tighten
  builder's-remedy misuse protections, unrelated to SB9 directly, reportedly failed to advance
  from Senate Housing Committee in April 2025 per search result — see Ballot/Ordinance section).
  Treat "no SB9 amendment found" as **absence of evidence**, not confirmed absence — worth a
  follow-up targeted search specifically on Leg Info for SB9-amending bills chaptered in 2025/2026.

### SB35 (streamlined ministerial approval)
- County is very likely still SB35-eligible in the "insufficient progress toward Above Moderate
  Income RHNA" category (San Mateo County was one of the counties named in HCD's determination
  methodology per search results), which triggers a **10% affordability threshold** SB35 pathway
  for the county's unincorporated area. **Not independently re-verified against the live current
  HCD determination table** — the HCD PDF fetched this pass returned corrupted/unreadable text;
  treat SB35-eligibility as **UNCONFIRMED at high-confidence-but-not-verified-live** and flag for
  a follow-up direct read of the HCD determination summary table.
- **Objective design standards:** county Planning Commission was presented (January 2026) with
  a new ordinance establishing objective design/development standards for Design Review zoning
  districts in unincorporated areas, repealing/replacing prior Zoning Regulations provisions —
  per Coastside Buzz, this differentiates **inland areas (design review with objective
  standards)** from the **Coastal Zone (still discretionary review, except ADUs which remain
  mandatory ministerial)**. This is a materially new item not in prior research — the county's
  objective-standards adoption is recent (early-to-mid 2026) and coastal-zone unincorporated
  areas (Midcoast, Devil's Slide, etc.) appear to remain under discretionary (non-objective)
  design review, which has SB35-eligibility implications for those specific coastal parcels.

### SB10 (local transit-adjacent upzoning, opt-in)
- **No evidence found that San Mateo County has adopted an SB10 ordinance or mapped any SB10
  parcels.** Search turned up only unrelated county ordinance-code and SB63 (transit sales-tax)
  results. Consistent with SB10 being opt-in and largely unused statewide by counties (most SB10
  activity nationally has been in a handful of cities, not counties). **Treat as: not adopted,
  reasonably confident, but absence-of-evidence rather than a definitive negative confirmation
  from a county ordinance index.**

## Recent Ballot / Ordinance Changes (2025–2026)

1. **Midcoast building-permit freeze (major, newly surfaced finding)** — The county's Local
   Coastal Program Policy 1.23 caps new residential building permits (including ADUs) in the
   Urban Midcoast (Montara/Moss Beach/El Granada/Princeton/Miramar) at **40 units/year**,
   averaged over a rolling 3-year window. In 2025 the county issued **102 units** (71 of them the
   Cypress Point affordable project + other approvals), which — to hold the 3-year average at
   40/year — leaves only **18 permits total for 2026 and 2027 combined**. Result: **the county
   has HALTED accepting new residential building-permit applications in the Midcoast, effective
   through January 1, 2028, or until an LCP amendment provides relief** (whichever comes first).
   A public meeting on a proposed LCP amendment to raise/modify the cap was held **June 30,
   2026**; any amendment still requires Planning Commission + Board of Supervisors approval AND
   **California Coastal Commission certification**, which the county itself estimates "could take
   a year or more." Sources: `smcgov.org/planning/news/urban-midcoast-building-permit-cap`,
   `.../urban-midcoast-building-permit-cap-waitlist`, San Mateo Daily Journal coverage (fetch
   blocked by 403 this pass — corroborated via search snippet only), Midcoast Community Council
   meeting page. **This is a structurally significant, not-previously-flagged finding** — see
   Contradictions section.
2. **New objective design standards ordinance for unincorporated Design Review districts** —
   presented to Planning Commission January 2026; applies inland; Coastal Zone unincorporated
   areas remain under discretionary review (ADUs excepted, still ministerial). Source: Coastside
   Buzz.
3. **Housing Element rezoning program (HE 11.2) amendment** — proposed March 2026 amendment
   raising max density from 120 to 150 units/acre for non-coastal rezoning-program sites, public
   review through March 19, 2026, adopted with the Housing Element April 21, 2026.
4. **October 8, 2024 County Ordinance Code restructuring** (carried over from prior research,
   reconfirmed as background) — repealed/replaced old Divisions VI/VII/VIII with new Title
   8 (Development Code) / Title 9 (Subdivision) / Title 10 (Building); Residential Hillside
   District is now Title 8 Ch. 8.110, ADUs allowed subject to ADU chapter.
5. **No countywide ballot measure specific to unincorporated-area housing growth control** was
   found for 2025 or 2026 (the Midcoast cap is a pre-existing certified-LCP policy being
   administered/amended administratively + through Coastal Commission process, not a new ballot
   measure). Ballotpedia's San Mateo County ballot-measures index was found but not exhaustively
   cross-checked line-by-line in this pass — flag as **not fully ruled out**.
6. **No North Fair Oaks-specific 2025-2026 zoning/ordinance activity** beyond the 2019 Community
   Plan was found in this pass.
7. A state Senate bill intended to curb Builder's Remedy misuse (protecting cities) reportedly
   **failed to advance from the Senate Housing Committee around April 29, 2025** (SD13 press
   release) — relevant context for why BR exposure/protections remain unchanged at the state
   level; not itself a county-level change.
