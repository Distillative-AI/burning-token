## 1. Current Compliance Status

- **Housing Element: CERTIFIED / COMPLIANT.** The City's 2023-2031 (6th Cycle) Housing Element was
  certified by California HCD on **March 20-21, 2024** (city press release dated 2024-03-21; cited
  in the City's own Nov. 4, 2025 staff report as "certified... March 20, 2024"). No decertification
  event was found as of Sept 2026 — Menlo Park remains in substantial compliance with state Housing
  Element law.
- The City submitted its General Plan/Housing Element Annual Progress Report (APR) to HCD by the
  **April 1, 2026** statutory deadline; presented to Planning Commission March 23, 2026 and City
  Council March 24, 2026.
- **Caveat / forward risk:** Menlo Park is subject to **SB 423** mid-cycle "builder's remedy
  extension" audits in **2027** if actual RHNA production (very-low/low-income units) falls short —
  reporting as of Dec 2025 flagged Menlo Park as one of several Peninsula cities behind on
  low-income unit production pace. This does not change 2026 compliance status but is a near-term
  risk flag.
- Despite Housing-Element *certification*, Menlo Park is simultaneously the subject of a **state
  Attorney General finding (July 29, 2026)** that it violated AB 2011 and the Housing Accountability
  Act in its *handling of a specific project* (80 Willow Road) — this is a project-level HAA/AB2011
  enforcement action, not a Housing Element decertification, but materially affects the city's
  legal exposure and Builder's Remedy leverage narrative.

## 2. SB9 (Lot Splits)

- **Locally adopted ordinance**, not just state-default eligibility: **Ordinance 1096**, adopted
  **March 28, 2023**, effective **April 27, 2023**. Codified as Menlo Park Municipal Code Ch.
  15.31 (Urban Lot Splits), Ch. 15.34 (Two-Unit Housing Developments), and Ch. 16.77.
- **Local modifications/restrictions confirmed:**
  - Urban-lot-split resulting uses limited to **residential only**.
  - **Short-term rentals prohibited** on SB9 lot-split/two-unit parcels.
  - Technical/objective standards: minimum panhandle width (20 ft) for irregular splits, separate
    utility connections (water/sewer/electrical) required per unit.
  - Ministerial review confirmed (no discretionary hearing) when objective standards are met.
  - **Live update in progress:** Planning Commission agendized "SB 9 Ordinance updates" on
    **April 20, 2026** — the ordinance was under active revision as of spring 2026; exact
    amendment text not yet pulled (see Unconfirmed section).

## 3. SB35 (Streamlined Ministerial Approval)

- Menlo Park is **RHNA-eligible-tier** for SB35 statewide (below the state's affordability-progress
  threshold in at least one income category historically), making it a plausible SB35 target city,
  but **no Menlo Park-specific adopted objective design standards ordinance for SB35 purposes was
  located** in this pass (searches returned only generic/statewide SB35 guidance and other cities'
  standards, e.g. Santa Barbara, Morgan Hill).
- **Live case law on SB35/AB2011 eligibility just occurred**: the 80 Willow Road (Willow Park)
  project was found by the City in **November 2025** to be **ineligible for streamlined
  ministerial approval / Builder's Remedy** per the City's own determination — a decision the state
  Attorney General's July 29, 2026 letter says was based on improperly shifting rationale and
  violates AB 2011 and the Housing Accountability Act. This is the single most consequential
  SB35/AB2011-adjacent fact pattern in the county this cycle: it is actively contested, unresolved
  as of Sept 2026, and heading toward litigation.

## 4. SB10 (Transit Density)

- **No evidence found that Menlo Park has adopted an SB10 ordinance** rezoning any parcel to up to
  10 units/parcel in a transit-rich/urban-infill area. Searches returned only general SB10 statutory
  background, not a Menlo Park-specific adoption. Treat Menlo Park as **not opted in to SB10** as of
  Sept 2026 (unconfirmed negative — a targeted council-agenda search for "SB10" specifically was not
  exhaustive; see Unconfirmed section).

## 6. 2025/2026 Anti-Housing Ballot Measure — Downtown Parking Lots

- **Now officially Measure P**, City of Menlo Park, **November 3, 2026 General Election**.
- Official city page: https://www.menlopark.gov/Government/Departments/City-Managers-Office/City-Clerk/Elections/Nov.-3-2026-General-Election/Measure-P
- **Ballot question (city's own language):** would prohibit the City from selling, trading,
  leasing, donating, disposing, or otherwise conveying all or a portion of City-owned downtown
  Menlo Park parking lots — for any reason that would affect or diminish availability/convenience
  of parking — **without voter approval**, with certain exceptions (maintenance, capital
  improvement, temporary events).
- **Council action:** City Council voted **unanimously** (Dec. 2, 2025 meeting) to place the
  citizen-initiated measure on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot (Option B under Elections Code §9215),
  rather than adopting it outright (Option A) or ordering a further §9212 impact report (Option C)
  — although the Council had earlier ordered/considered a ~$165,000 impact study, with Mayor Drew
  Combs the sole dissenting vote on delaying via that study ("kicking the can down the road").
- **Signature drive:** ~15% of registered voters signed (city requires 10% to qualify).
- **Status as of Sept 5, 2026 (today):** Measure P is **qualified and set for the Nov. 3, 2026
  ballot; the vote has NOT yet occurred** — this is a pending, not resolved, ballot item. No lawsuit
  over the measure itself was found in this pass (the active litigation risk in Menlo Park housing
  is the separate 80 Willow Road/AB 712 matter, not Measure P).
- **Direct housing linkage (unchanged from prior research):** blocks execution of Housing Element
  **Program H4.G** (≥345 affordable units planned across the downtown parking lots) without a
  separate voter-approval step.
