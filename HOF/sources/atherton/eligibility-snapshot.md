## Current Compliance Status (Housing Element)

**Compliant since May 5, 2025.** HCD certified the Town of Atherton's 6th-cycle (2023-2031) Housing
Element on May 5, 2025, after rejecting two prior draft submissions. The certified version was
submitted March 6, 2025, following City Council-approved changes on February 24, 2025.

Atherton was **non-compliant from January 31, 2023** (the statutory 6th-cycle deadline) **through
May 4, 2025** — roughly 2 years, 3 months of non-compliance / builder's-remedy exposure. During
that non-compliance window Atherton received **zero** builder's remedy applications, per the
Town's own statements to press.

RHNA allocation: 348 new units by 2031. Per a January 2025 Almanac article the state and Housing
Leadership Council of San Mateo County criticized Atherton's draft for leaning on what advocates
called "infeasible" ADU-heavy proposals (town projected ~26 ADUs/year, 208 units total from ADUs
alone) rather than rezoning for multifamily.

HCD's statewide Housing Element Implementation and APR dashboard was not directly reachable via
fetch in this pass (PDF/portal parsing issues) — compliance date is corroborated via contemporaneous
local press (The Almanac) rather than a direct HCD portal screenshot. Recommend a follow-up direct
check of https://www.hcd.ca.gov/planning-and-community-development/housing-open-data-tools/housing-element-implementation-and-apr-dashboard
to confirm current-as-of-Sept-2026 status is still "in compliance" (no decertification since).

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

**SB9 (lot splits):** Atherton processes SB9 urban lot splits via a Town-adopted tentative-map
checklist/process (see athertonca.gov/640/SB-9-Urban-Lot-Split), i.e. it has local
implementing procedure rather than pure ad hoc state-default handling, though it must still apply
only objective standards per state law. Applications have "trickled in" since 2022 (first approved
lot split: 2 Lowery Drive). Atherton's zoning is dominated by large minimum lot sizes (commonly
1-acre-plus estate zoning), which interacts with SB9's minimum-lot-size (2,400 sq ft post-split)
rule to somewhat limit how many parcels qualify, but applications and approvals have occurred.

**SB9 litigation:** A group of CALIFORNIA CHARTER CITIES (not directly confirmed to include
Atherton — Atherton is a general-law town, not a charter city, which is directly relevant: the
2024 LA Superior Court ruling that struck down SB9 as applied to certain CHARTER cities on home-rule
grounds would likely **not** extend to Atherton, since Atherton is a general-law town subject to
state law as a matter of statewide concern regardless of that ruling). UNCONFIRMED: could not
verify from a readable source whether Atherton was itself a named plaintiff in any SB9 challenge;
the Superior Court case identified in search results concerned five (charter) cities and Atherton's
status as a general-law town was not resolved in the sources fetched. Flagging as UNCONFIRMED.

**Notable 2025 development — charter-city proposal aimed partly at land-use control:** In June 2025,
an Atherton City Council subcommittee floated pursuing **charter city status**, partly to enable a
real-estate transfer tax (~1%, ~$4M/year) and partly for greater local land-use control, explicitly
naming SB9 as a law charter status *might* help resist — while acknowledging in the same report that
many state housing statutes declare themselves matters of "statewide concern" and would bind charter
cities too. Council discussed a possible **November 2026 ballot measure** asking voters to approve
charter city status. This is exploratory as of the June 16, 2025 article, not yet adopted or
qualified for the ballot as of the source date. STATUS AS OF SEPT 2026: UNCONFIRMED whether this
measure has since been formally qualified/placed on the November 2026 ballot — needs a follow-up
check closer to filing deadlines.

**SB35:** Atherton is listed among jurisdictions **NOT** currently subject to SB35 streamlined
ministerial provisions per HCD determination data referenced in search results (this is consistent
with Atherton's now-compliant Housing Element status — SB35 eligibility/streamlining generally
applies where a jurisdiction is behind on RHNA production, and the applicable affordability tier
depends on progress toward above-moderate vs. lower-income RHNA). No SB35 projects identified as
filed or approved in Atherton.

**SB10:** No evidence found that Atherton has adopted a local SB10 resolution/ordinance mapping
transit-rich or jobs-rich parcels for up to 10 units/parcel. Given Atherton's zoning profile (no
significant transit corridors, no job centers) and general resistance posture toward densification,
this is very unlikely; treating as **no adoption**, but note this is an absence-of-evidence
inference, not a directly confirmed "council voted no" record — flagged as reasonably confident,
not certain.
