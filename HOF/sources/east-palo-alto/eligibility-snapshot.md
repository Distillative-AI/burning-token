# East Palo Alto — Builder's Remedy / SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Eligibility Snapshot

Compiled 2026-09-05. San Mateo County, CA.

## Current Compliance Status (Housing Element)

East Palo Alto's 2023-2031 (6th Cycle) Housing Element is **CERTIFIED / COMPLIANT** with HCD.

Timeline:
- Council initially adopted the Housing Element on **2023-02-21**.
- HCD found the Revised Housing Element in substantial compliance (pending council adoption) on **2024-02-20**.
- City Council adopted the Revised Housing Element and transmitted to HCD on **2024-03-19**.
- HCD certified the Revised Housing Element as substantially compliant on **2024-04-29**.

This implies a **non-compliance / builder's-remedy-exposed window roughly 2023-02-21 through 2024-04-29** (~14 months) during which the city's Housing Element had not yet been certified by HCD. UNCONFIRMED whether the city was formally deemed "non-compliant" by HCD during that exact window versus simply "in process" — the certification-dashboard record itself was not directly loaded (see Unconfirmed section).

As of September 2026, no source found indicates decertification or a compliance challenge since April 2024 — status is treated as **currently compliant**.

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

**SB9 (lot splits):**
- No evidence found of a local SB9 implementing ordinance with additional objective standards, owner-occupancy requirements, or historic-district exclusions.
- City's own SB9/HOME Act page and handout describe only the state-default ministerial process (up to two primary units and/or one lot split), plus standard eligibility screens (environmental site constraints, anti-displacement/tenancy protections required by state law itself, not added local restrictions).
- Working conclusion: East Palo Alto appears to be **relying on the state-default SB9 process**, not a bespoke local ordinance. UNCONFIRMED against the municipal code directly (Municode East Palo Alto codes were not fetched/read in this pass).

**SB35 (streamlined ministerial approval):**
- East Palo Alto **is on HCD's SB35 determination list**, at the **10% affordability tier** (city's own page states: "The City of East Palo Alto's SB35 determination is at 10% and we are subject to the SB35 guidelines for streamlined approvals").
- Two projects identified by the city's SB35 page as seeking streamlined approval:
  - **Weeks Street Townhomes** — 760 Weeks Street, East Palo Alto, CA 94303
  - **1804 Bay Road Mixed-Use Development** — 1804 Bay Road, East Palo Alto, CA 94303
  - No filing dates, unit counts, or approval status given on the page for either project — UNCONFIRMED, needs direct planning-department follow-up.
- Note: SB35's original sunset (Jan 1, 2026) was **removed/extended by SB423 (2023)**, which also expanded coverage to coastal zones — so SB35/423 streamlining is understood to remain active past Jan 2026, not sunsetting on schedule as an initial search snippet suggested (that snippet appears to describe the pre-SB423 sunset date, now superseded).

**SB10 (up to 10 units/parcel, transit-rich/jobs-rich rezoning):**
- **No evidence of East Palo Alto adopting a local SB10 resolution or ordinance.** The city's own "State Housing Bills" summary page (covering SB9, SB35/423, SB330, SB684, SB1123, SB4) **does not mention SB10 at all**.
- Consistent with the general statewide pattern (most CA cities have not adopted SB10, since it is opt-in and requires an affirmative city ordinance/council vote by 2029 sunset).
- Conclusion: **Zero confirmed SB10 adoption in East Palo Alto** as of September 2026. This is a confirmed absence, not an assumption, based on the city's own bill-tracking page being silent on it.

## Recent Changes (2025-2026 ballot measures/ordinances)

- **2025-09-03**: City Council voted to allow the "Four Corners" project (Sand Hill Property Co., 1675 Bay Road) to reduce/eliminate very-low-income inclusionary units — a council vote, not a ballot measure, affecting a specific project's affordability terms.
- **2025-10** (approx.): City contributed to a county-wide affordable housing feasibility study (cited as background for the incentive-program proposal below).
- **2025-12**: Mayor Webster Lincoln proposed a "Temporary Housing Development Incentive Program" that would have exempted projects of ≤20 units from the city's standard 20% inclusionary requirement (affordable to households at 35-60% AMI, ~$114,000).
- **2026-03-28**: Reported publicly as "East Palo Alto looks to limit affordable housing standards in new law" (Palo Alto Online).
- **2026-07-22**: City Council **rejected/killed** the Temporary Housing Development Incentive Program at the last minute, after community opposition over displacement concerns; Council member Martha Barragan reversed her initial support ("The people have spoken"). Program would have applied to only ~128 of 3,966 planned units (~3%) and would have eliminated an estimated 17 affordable units / ~$2M in foregone in-lieu fees.
- No ballot-measure (voter initiative/referendum) changes to the housing approval process were found for 2025-2026 — the above were council-level ordinance votes, not ballot measures. East Palo Alto does have a pre-existing, voter-approved **Rent Stabilization and Just Cause for Eviction Ordinance** (passed by 79% of voters, year UNCONFIRMED in this pass, predates 2025), currently allowing a 2.2% rent increase for program year July 2025–June 2026 — this is a rent-control ordinance, not a zoning/entitlement-process change, and not itself newly enacted in 2025-2026.
