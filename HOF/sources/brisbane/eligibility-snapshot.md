# Brisbane, CA — Builder's Remedy / SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Eligibility Snapshot

Research date: 2026-09-05. Status reflects information available as of that date.

## Current Compliance Status (Housing Element)

**NON-COMPLIANT as of July 1, 2026.**

- Brisbane's 6th Cycle (2023-2031) Housing Element was originally certified by HCD in 2023.
- The certified Housing Element committed the city to adopting a Specific Plan for the
  Baylands subarea (1,800–2,200 housing units on the northwest quadrant of the ~660-acre
  Baylands site), which accounts for roughly 81% of Brisbane's total RHNA allocation of
  1,588 units for the cycle.
- The Housing Element set a state-law-imposed deadline of **May 18, 2026** for the city to
  certify the Baylands EIR and adopt the Baylands Specific Plan.
- Brisbane missed that deadline. The Planning Commission held public hearings on the
  Specific Plan on June 25, June 30, and July 23, 2026, closing the public hearing on
  July 23, 2026 (per one source) / continuing deliberation to August 13, 2026 (per
  another source) — sequencing between these two reports is not fully reconciled (see
  Unconfirmed section).
- On **July 1, 2026**, HCD rescinded its compliance finding and formally decertified
  Brisbane's Housing Element, per a letter from HCD Housing Accountability Chief Melinda
  Coy to Brisbane officials. Brisbane is reported to be one of only three California
  jurisdictions (along with Portola Valley and Norwalk) to have had a previously
  certified Housing Element subsequently decertified.
- Effect: Brisbane loses local land-use discretion over qualifying housing projects and
  becomes ineligible for certain state housing-related funding streams while
  non-compliant.
- The city states it anticipates recertification once the Baylands Specific Plan is
  adopted, but no recertification date is confirmed as of this research.
- Could not independently load/verify Brisbane's row on HCD's own Housing Element
  Implementation and APR Dashboard (page did not return parseable compliance-status data
  via fetch) — status above is corroborated instead via city and press sources. See
  Unconfirmed section.

## SB9 / SB35 / SB10 Status

**SB9 (lot splits):**
- Brisbane has pursued a **local implementing ordinance** with objective development
  standards (not relying solely on state-default ministerial process), per its own FAQ
  and draft-ordinance materials.
- Draft ordinance objective standards identified:
  - **Building height**: in the zone between the district's standard setback and the
    state's mandated 4-foot setback allowance, height is capped at 25 feet; the same
    25-foot cap applies to ridgeline lots within the R-BA district.
  - **Parking**: consistent with SB9's own cap, no more than one parking space per
    primary dwelling unit may be required.
- Could not confirm whether an owner-occupancy requirement or historic-district
  exclusion is part of Brisbane's adopted ordinance — sources found describe only the
  height/parking objective standards from draft/Planning Commission materials, not a
  confirmed final adopted ordinance text or date of adoption.

**SB35 (streamlined ministerial approval):**
- General SB35 mechanics confirmed (HCD determines subject jurisdictions based on RHNA
  progress vs. APR data; two affordability tiers, 10% and 50%).
- Could NOT independently confirm Brisbane's specific listing/tier on HCD's SB35
  determination list/map (data.ca.gov "Streamlined Ministerial Approval Process (SB35)
  Determination Map" dataset) — the dataset page was located but not queried for
  Brisbane's specific row.
- No news coverage found of any SB35 project actually filed or approved in Brisbane.

**SB10 (up to 10 units/parcel on transit-rich/jobs-rich parcels):**
- **No evidence found of Brisbane adopting a local SB10 resolution or ordinance.**
  SB10 is opt-in only (a city must affirmatively pass a resolution); nothing in city
  council agendas, minutes, or news coverage surfaced by this research indicates Brisbane
  has done so. Treat as **zero adoption**, consistent with the fact that most CA cities
  have not adopted SB10 — but this is an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed
  negative from an HCD SB10 tracker (HCD does not appear to maintain a public SB10
  adoption list the way it does for SB35).

## Recent Changes (2025-2026 ballot measures/ordinances)

- **2018 Measure JJ** (historical, not 2025-2026): general plan amendment approved by
  Brisbane voters allowing 1,800–2,200 homes and up to 6.5M sq ft of commercial space at
  Baylands — the underlying entitlement basis for the current Specific Plan process.
  Included for context only; predates the research window.
- **Draft EIR** for the Baylands Specific Plan was circulated for public review/comment
  over a 151-day period from April 2025 to September 2025.
- **Final EIR** published the week of May 11, 2026.
- **Planning Commission hearings** on the Specific Plan held June 25, June 30, and
  (per one source) July 23, 2026; another source states the Commission continued
  deliberation to August 13, 2026 — see Unconfirmed section for the discrepancy.
- **No ballot measure or referendum specific to 2025-2026** affecting Brisbane's housing
  approval process was found (Measure JJ, above, is from 2018 and not a 2025-2026 event).
- **July 1, 2026**: HCD decertification (treated above as the single most consequential
  "process change" in the window, since it is a state action, not a local ordinance, but
  it is the dominant driver of Brisbane's current approval-process posture).
