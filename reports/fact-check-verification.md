## Summary

- **Confirmed (fully or in substance): 6** — CARLA/HAA settlement (#1), non-compliance rate (#2, aggregate only), RHNA 6th-cycle total (#3, headline number), Measure T (#5), median household income (#9), Redwood City 603 Jefferson (#10).
- **Outdated / needs updating: 3** — the 17:1 jobs-housing ratio (#4, a recycled 2010-2016-era stat, not a current measurement), the Menlo Park ballot measure's date label (#6, now a Nov. 2026 ballot item, not "2025"), and the Pacifica Quarry unit count/status (#7, now 1,225 units and **rejected 5-0 by the Planning Commission on Aug 3**, not a clean active pipeline example).
- **Unverifiable in this pass: 2** (partial) — the exact 8-jurisdiction non-compliance roster (#2, aggregate rate confirmed but the specific list could not be independently pinned to a single current source), and the SSF $424,840.11/unit in-lieu fee (#8, right source identified but exact figure not independently confirmed).

## Single most important correction

**The Pacifica Quarry project (#7) is the most consequential fix.** The original report cites it as a live example of Builder's Remedy successfully forcing a thousand-unit-scale project through the pipeline. In fact, as of the most recent available reporting, Pacifica's Planning Commission voted 5-0 to *reject* the (now 1,225-unit) application, explicitly arguing that state housing law does not override the city's voter-approved land-use requirements — directly contesting the core Builder's Remedy mechanism the report leans on as its highest-confidence example. This changes the project from "proof Builder's Remedy works" to "an active legal fight over whether it works," which materially affects the report's Section 4 ranked-intervention argument (#2: "Aggressive Builder's Remedy enforcement ... Pacifica, Menlo Park already show thousand-unit-scale pipelines result" — that claim is now contested, not settled, at the anchor jurisdiction cited).

A secondary but broader concern: the widely-repeated **17:1 jobs-housing ratio (#4)** is a decade-old statistic still being cited as current across multiple outlets (including the report's own ABC7 source) — this is a pattern worth flagging generally: "least compliant" / "worst imbalance" framings in this space often rest on stale but frequently-recycled numbers, and a rigorous report should either recompute the ratio from current EDD/Census data or explicitly caveat the vintage of the figure.
